\title{CARE: Repair Before You Decide in Lossy Multi-Robot Map Sharing}
\author{Anonymous Authors}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Sparse remote map updates can be lost precisely when a robot must commit to an
uncertain route. We formulate replica repair as encoded-byte minimization under
a planning deadline and introduce CARE, a versioned anti-entropy protocol that
derives both quantities from a receiver's local planning problem. Optimistic
and pessimistic paths identify an ambiguous branch; the first unknown cell on
the operational path gives the latest safe repair time, and the one-hop
action-graph influence set gives the cells to query. On 100 independently
generated matched maps with 30\% loss, CARE improves A* completion success
from 0.8400 to 0.9375 for 3.28~KB additional attempted traffic over one-shot
sharing. It matches the reliability of the earlier fixed-corridor PSR-UT while
using 7.15~KB less traffic. Repeating the full matrix with incremental D* Lite
gives 0.9400 success and the same traffic operating point. Delay experiments
show that CARE suppresses repairs whose query--patch round trip cannot meet the
derived deadline.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
multi-robot systems, map sharing, lossy communication, replicated state,

\section{Introduction}
Robots with local sensing often exchange map state. A dropped cell update can
leave one receiver with a stale replica; if the cell lies on an imminent route,
the receiver may commit before local sensing resolves the uncertainty. This is
not a general claim that every replica mismatch matters. Rather, it is a
specific failure mode of one-shot explicit map sharing under a decision
deadline.

We study an explicit-state systems question instead of replacing the planner or
learning a latent communication policy. Existing learned communication systems
optimize different representations and coordination objectives
\cite{ma2021dcc,wang2023scrimp}. Here all methods share the cell representation,
binary codec, instance, budgets, and lossy trace; only missing-state recovery
changes. A* and D* Lite are evaluated independently to separate the protocol
claim from one planner's tie breaking.

Our contributions are: (i) an encoded-byte objective subject to a local,
truth-map-free decision certificate; (ii) a deadline derived from route
commitment and measured query--patch latency; (iii) a graph-derived adaptive
repair scope rather than a fixed corridor rule; and (iv) matched 100-map
evidence across static and incremental planners, including traffic,
reliability, delay, and computation overhead.

\section{Replica Repair Protocol}
Each robot maintains a versioned cell map $M_i^t$. A cell carries a
lexicographically ordered stamp containing version, observation time, sender,
and state tie break. An incoming cell is merged only if its stamp dominates the
local stamp. Normal observations are directed one-shot deltas. Data, digest,
acknowledgement, and repair bytes all traverse the same directed lossy channel.

We use an executable fixed-width wire format rather than object-level traffic
estimates. A cell delta is 13 bytes, including provenance, coordinates, state,
version, observation time, and CRC16. A query over $n$ stamped cells is
$16+6n$ bytes, a patch returning $m$ cells is $4+13m$ bytes, an ACK is 8 bytes,
and a replica digest is 16 bytes. The six-byte query entry bit-packs bounded
coordinates and the complete requester stamp. Update identifiers are
deterministically reconstructed and are not transmitted. The channel carries
the resulting byte strings; decoders reject invalid lengths, versions, bounds,
reserved bits, or checksums before applying the same dominating-stamp merge.
All attempted encoded bytes, including lost packets, count as traffic.

For receiver $j$, let $\pi_j^+$ be its operational optimistic path and
$\pi_j^-$ the path obtained when UNKNOWN cells are blocked. Different action
sequences indicate a robust-decision ambiguity without accessing map truth.
Let $k_j$ be the index of the first UNKNOWN cell on $\pi_j^+$. The last safe
repair time before entering uncertain state is
\begin{equation}
\tau_j=\max(0,k_j-1), \qquad L_{\rm rtt}=2d,
\end{equation}
where $d$ is the measured one-way link delay in environment steps. CARE sends
only if $L_{\rm rtt}\leq\tau_j$.

To derive scope, CARE follows both paths from first divergence to first
reconvergence. For the next route commitment, direct branch feasibility is
determined by branch vertices and their one-step successors. CARE therefore
forms $\mathcal{S}_j$ from UNKNOWN vertices on or incident to those branches in
the four-neighbor action graph. Incidence is fixed by the planner's motion model,
not a tuned dilation radius. With the executable
codec, query cost is $B(Q)=16+6|Q|$. CARE solves
\begin{equation}
\min_{Q\subseteq\mathcal{S}_j} B(Q)
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad
\mathcal{S}_j\subseteq Q,\; L_{\rm rtt}\leq\tau_j,
\end{equation}
whose feasible minimum is $Q^*=\mathcal{S}_j$; otherwise $Q^*=\varnothing$.
If the codec cap binds, witnesses are retained in path order, protecting the
earliest decisions first. A peer returns only cells with dominating stamps.
This is a computable certificate under local interval uncertainty, not an
oracle assumption that a peer knows every requested cell.

\section{Experimental Protocol}
The primary task is an eight-robot shared-map decision geometry: remote
observers can see a blocked branch before paired seekers reach a fork, while
seekers cannot locally observe the branch before committing. Every comparison
uses 100 independent maps, each paired across policies with one independent
loss trace and identical per-step budgets. The complete policy matrix is run
once with deterministic A* and once with incremental D* Lite. We report
trial-level means, sample standard deviations, and bootstrap 95\% intervals
over maps.

Primary controls are One-shot Unique Delta, Retry-All ARQ, Path-Weighted ARQ,
Periodic Full Anti-Entropy (K=4), Mismatch-Triggered Full Replica Repair, and
PSR-UT, and CARE. The ablations change one
factor: utility trigger versus an action-triggered repair control, cadence 1
versus 4, and corridor repair versus full-replica repair. Extensions sweep
loss, obstacle density, link delay, agent/map scale, and three independent
decision geometries. Random-map conditions are retained as negative controls.

\section{Results}
At 30\% loss with A*, CARE improves One-shot completion success by $+0.0975$
(paired 95\% CI $[+0.0800,+0.1163]$) for 3.28~KB additional attempted bytes.
Relative to fixed-corridor PSR-UT, CARE changes success by $+0.0050$
($[-0.0125,+0.0225]$) while saving 7.15~KB
($[-7.39,-6.91]$). D* Lite independently yields $+0.0938$ over One-shot and
$+0.0088$ over PSR-UT, while saving 7.03~KB versus PSR-UT. Thus the protocol
gain is stable to planner substitution. CARE does not claim universal
dominance: under A*, Retry-All reaches 0.9762 CSR at 263.9~KB and Periodic Full
reaches 0.9575 at 285.8~KB; CARE is the 0.9375/68.6~KB operating point.

The original ablations motivate the upgrade. Replacing PSR-UT with action-triggered
repair reduces CSR from 0.9325 to 0.8400 ($+0.0925$ for PSR-UT,
$[+0.0750,+0.1100]$). Changing only the repair cadence from every step to
every four steps reduces CSR to 0.8488, exposing the decision deadline.
Changing only the repair scope to a full replica reduces CSR to 0.8400 and
increases traffic from 75.8 to 174.0~KB; paired changes are $+0.0925$ CSR and
$-98.2$~KB in favor of PSR-UT.

The deadline experiment supplies the new causal check. CARE's mean derived
slack is about 4.1 steps. With one-step delay it obtains 0.9000 A* CSR; at
two-step delay, about half the candidate repairs remain feasible and CARE
matches PSR-UT at 0.8550 while saving 11.78~KB. At four-step delay no query can
complete before commitment: CARE sends no repair, matches One-shot and PSR-UT
at 0.7388, and saves 17.35~KB versus PSR-UT. Planning computation is
42.14~ms per A* episode (37.78~ms for One-shot) and 156.02~ms per D* Lite
episode (142.77~ms for One-shot); D* Lite is used as an independence check,
not claimed as the faster implementation on these small maps.

The prior extensions delimit the task regime. PSR-UT achieves CSR 0.9913, 0.9750,
0.9325, 0.9175, and 0.8388 at loss probabilities 0.1 through 0.5. At one,
two, and four delivery steps, CSR decreases to 0.9075, 0.8550, and 0.7388,
respectively. Across 4/8/16/32 agents, its paired improvement over One-shot
is positive but small: $+0.0050$ to $+0.0003$. It improves over One-shot in
T-junction, asymmetric-fork, and narrow-bypass validations by $+0.1163$,
$+0.1175$, and $+0.1275$ CSR. In random-map negative controls, PSR-UT and
One-shot are approximately equivalent. These controls show that replica repair
matters when a missing update changes an imminent route, not for arbitrary map
disagreement.

\section{Conclusion}
CARE turns decision-critical replica repair into a codec-priced constrained
communication algorithm. Receiver-local path bounds identify ambiguity, route
commitment derives the deadline, and the planner action graph derives a compact
repair certificate. Matched A* and D* Lite results support a planner-independent
reliability--traffic operating point; delay experiments expose exactly when
repair becomes too late. Reliable ARQ remains the higher-traffic reliability
endpoint rather than a target CARE claims to dominate.
