\title{CARE: Repair Before You Decide in Lossy Multi-Robot Map Sharing}
\author{Anonymous Authors}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Sparse remote map updates can be lost exactly when a robot must commit to an
uncertain route. We formulate replica repair as encoded-byte minimization under
a planning deadline and introduce Commitment-Aware Replica rEpair (CARE), a
versioned anti-entropy protocol that computes a minimum decision certificate.
CARE enumerates a bounded sparse occupancy-scenario family and solves an exact
hitting-set problem whose queried cells distinguish all still-repairable action
classes. A route-commitment certificate accounts for query--patch latency. We
evaluate 59,000 episodes, each condition using 100 physically distinct maps
paired across methods. In the primary 5$\times$5-sensing condition at 30\%
loss, CARE improves completion success from 0.6713 to 0.7362 with A* and from
0.6900 to 0.7688 with D* Lite, adding only 5.63/5.47~KB over one-shot sharing.
Path-aware heuristics recover most of this gain. Relative to a matched
path-only Top-K repair, CARE gains 0.0088/0.0075 success while sending about
1.1~KB less; relative to a stronger single-cell decision test, the reliability
difference is not statistically detected. Sensor, delay, topology, random-map,
and compute controls delimit the claim. A matched endpoint study places CARE
between zero-byte local sensing and continuous full synchronization: at
5$\times$5, full sync sends 891.80~KB versus CARE's 53.25~KB without a detected
success advantage. CARE is a bounded joint-scenario certificate and selective
reliability point, not a universal replacement for reliable delivery.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
Robots with local sensing often exchange explicit map state. A dropped cell
update can leave one receiver with a stale replica; when that cell determines
an imminent branch, the receiver may commit before its own sensor resolves the
uncertainty. Conventional choices either tolerate the inconsistency, retry all
updates, or synchronize whole replicas periodically. They do not ask which
missing cells can still change a decision and whether a repair can arrive in
time.

We study this explicit-state recovery problem rather than replace the motion
planner or learn a latent communication policy. Learned communication systems
such as DCC and SCRIMP optimize different representations and coordination
objectives \cite{ma2021dcc,wang2023scrimp}. Generic replica reconciliation
instead tracks version progress, localizes differences with Merkle trees, or
decodes compressed set differences
\cite{vanrenesse2008scuttlebutt,decandia2007dynamo,goodrich2011iblt}. Here every method shares the same
cell codec, map, planner interface, loss trace, delay and byte accounting; only
replica recovery changes. We also repeat the main comparison with both A* and
incremental D* Lite, separating the communication claim from a planner's cache
or tie breaking.

Our contributions are: (i) a codec-priced optimization with an exact
scenario-separation condition; (ii) a dual certificate combining action
identifiability with a deadline derived from route commitment and measured
query--patch latency; (iii) a bounded online solver with explicit complexity
and guarantee limits; and (iv) a 59,000-episode evaluation with paired
map-cluster inference, three published reconciliation baselines, two matched
task-aware heuristics, controlled baselines and full-method ablations, two
planners, three sensing ranges, loss/delay sweeps, density/topology/scale
extensions, and random-map negative controls.

\section{Decision-Critical Replica Repair}
Each robot maintains a versioned occupancy map $M_i^t$. A deterministic stamp
orders version, observation time, sender and state tie break; an incoming cell
is merged only when its stamp dominates the local copy. Normal observations
are directed one-shot deltas, except in the Scuttlebutt adaptation where its
version-gossip exchange is the dissemination mechanism. Data, query, patch,
acknowledgement and digest packets traverse the same directed lossy channel.

The simulator uses an executable fixed-width wire format. A cell delta is 13
bytes, a query over $n$ stamped cells is $16+6n$ bytes, a patch returning $m$
cells is $4+13m$ bytes, an ACK is 8 bytes and a replica digest is 16 bytes.
Decoders validate lengths, versions, bounds, reserved bits and checksums. All
attempted encoded bytes, including lost packets, count as traffic.

Let $U_j$ contain at most $n=8$ UNKNOWN vertices on, or one graph hop from,
receiver $j$'s next $H=8$ optimistic path vertices. Scenario
$\omega\subseteq U_j$ marks blocked candidates. We enumerate
$\Omega_q=\{\omega:|\omega|\le q\}$ with $q=2$, giving at most
$S=\sum_{r=0}^q{n\choose r}=37$ scenarios. A canonical shortest-path oracle
evaluates each blocked counterfactual on the planner-shared action graph and
returns action sequence $\pi_\omega$. For two different sequences, let
$\delta(\omega,\omega')$ be their first divergence and
$D_{\omega\omega'}=\omega\triangle\omega'$. With one-way delay $d$, pairs
whose divergence occurs before the query--patch round trip $L_{\rm rtt}=2d$
cannot be repaired and are excluded. The remaining conflicts form
$\mathcal E_j$. CARE solves
\begin{equation}
\min_{Q\subseteq U_j} 16+6|Q|\quad\text{s.t.}\quad
Q\cap D_e\ne\varnothing,\quad \forall e\in\mathcal E_j .
\label{eq:hitting}
\end{equation}

\textit{Proposition.} $Q$ identifies every feasible action class in
$\Omega_q$ iff it satisfies (\ref{eq:hitting}). Query observations under two
scenarios are identical iff $Q$ misses their symmetric difference; therefore
hitting every action-conflicting pair is necessary and sufficient. Since every
entry costs six bytes, minimum cardinality is minimum encoded query size.
Cardinality-ordered enumeration is exact with bounded cost
$O(SP+S^2H+2^n|\mathcal E_j|n)$, where $P$ is one bounded path search.

Latency also imposes a route-entry deadline. Let $\pi_j^+$ traverse UNKNOWN
cells, $\pi_j^-$ block them, and $k_j$ be the first UNKNOWN index on
$\pi_j^+$. The safe slack is $\tau_j=\max(0,k_j-1)$. When
$L_{\rm rtt}\le\tau_j$, CARE unions the exact scenario certificate with a
one-hop influence certificate between the optimistic and pessimistic paths'
divergence and reconvergence; otherwise repair is suppressed. A peer returns
only cells with dominating stamps. The guarantee is exact only inside the
declared bounded $q$-sparse family. CARE neither observes ground truth nor
assumes a peer knows every queried cell.

\section{Experimental Protocol}
The controlled task contains eight robots and decision-critical forks: remote
observers see a blocked branch before paired seekers commit. The primary
sensor footprint is 5$\times$5. Every condition uses 100 physically distinct
layouts, paired across policies with the same deterministic packet-loss trace.
We report means, sample standard deviations and deterministic 20,000-draw
map-cluster bootstrap 95\% confidence intervals (CIs). Paired comparisons
subtract outcomes within the same layout and trace before resampling.

The main 9,600-episode matrix crosses six loss rates (0--0.5), eight policies
and A*/D* Lite. Controls are One-shot Unique Delta, Retry-All ARQ,
Path-Weighted ARQ, Periodic Full Sync ($K=4$), Mismatch-Triggered Full Repair,
fixed-corridor PSR-UT, rule-based CARE-Lite, and CARE. A separate
6,000-episode sensor study crosses 3$\times$3/5$\times$5/7$\times$7 sensing,
ten policies and both planners. Delay uses 3,200 episodes. The remaining
12,000 episodes cover matched full-method ablations, obstacle density,
decision topology, 4--32 robots, and random maps as negative controls.

A 21,600-episode external-baseline matrix crosses the three sensing ranges,
six losses, both planners and Scuttlebutt-Depth, Dynamo-style 16-ary Merkle
anti-entropy, 16-partition IBLT, One-shot, Retry-All and CARE. Scuttlebutt uses
per-origin version maxima and depth ordering; Merkle uses acknowledged,
loss-retried depth-first traversal; IBLT rotates 491-byte sketches through a
complete peer session. None receives path, goal or certificate information.

The closest 4,800-episode comparison holds the query--patch contract fixed and
crosses three sensing ranges, both planners, eight policies and 100 maps.
Path-Aware Top-K ranks UNKNOWN cells on or one hop from the next eight path
vertices by earliest path influence, querying up to the common eight-cell
(64-byte) cap without replanning. Single-Cell Decision Sensitivity independently
blocks each candidate, performs one canonical replan and ranks cells whose
action sequence diverges no earlier than the measured round trip. It neither
enumerates joint scenarios nor solves a hitting set. Thus these baselines test
whether gains require merely path information, independent counterfactuals, or
CARE's joint action-class certificate.

Finally, a 1,800-episode information-spectrum matrix compares No
Communication, CARE and Continuous Full Sync at all three sensing ranges and
both planners. No Communication suppresses every packet and is audited at zero
attempted bytes. Continuous Full Sync replaces ordinary deltas every step
($K=1$) with a rotating full-replica transfer. It uses the same lossy link,
13-byte cell codec, sender data cap and attempted-byte accounting as CARE; it
is not an idealized perfect-information channel. All summaries include mean
episode length (EL), SD and CI, and paired comparisons include $\Delta$EL.

We additionally record the peer's first observation of the critical obstacle,
the seeker's first local observation, route commitment, the signed repair
window, and candidate-cap saturation. Conditional timing means always include
event coverage. Independent reruns of overlapping 5$\times$5 conditions agree
on every non-timing output field.

\section{Results}
Table~\ref{tab:primary} shows the primary task-aware ladder. CARE improves A* success over
One-shot by $+0.0650$ (paired 95\% CI $[+0.0488,+0.0813]$; standardized paired
effect $d_z=0.789$) for $+5.63$~KB. With D* Lite the gain is $+0.0788$
($[+0.0612,+0.0963]$, $d_z=0.875$) for $+5.47$~KB. Relative to Retry-All,
CARE saves 101.20/101.75~KB but yields 0.0225/0.0175 lower success. Thus CARE
occupies a low-traffic point rather than the maximum-reliability endpoint.
Compared with PSR-UT and CARE-Lite, CARE is statistically indistinguishable in
success while saving about 9.5 and 3.8~KB, respectively.
Path-Aware Top-K recovers $+0.0563$ A* and $+0.0712$ D* Lite success over
One-shot without counterfactual planning. CARE exceeds it by only
$+0.0088$ ($[+0.0013,+0.0175]$) and $+0.0075$
($[+0.0000,+0.0150]$), while sending 1.12/1.13~KB less. Single-Cell
Sensitivity is stronger per byte: CARE--Single-Cell is $+0.0037$
($[-0.0037,+0.0125]$) and $+0.0063$ ($[-0.0013,+0.0150]$), with CARE sending
1.14/1.03~KB more. Hence path awareness explains most of the improvement; the
exact certificate contributes a declared joint-scenario guarantee and some
compression relative to broad path repair, but not detected reliability
dominance over every task-aware heuristic.
Among published methods, Scuttlebutt is strongest: CARE improves over it by
$+0.0238$ ($[+0.0088,+0.0387]$) with A* and $+0.0400$
($[+0.0225,+0.0575]$) with D* Lite while saving 26.90/27.54~KB. CARE also
exceeds Merkle by 0.0650/0.0788 and IBLT by 0.0550/0.0725, saving
19.93--99.62~KB.

gains $+0.2362$ ($[+0.2175,+0.2537]$) A* and $+0.2687$

Across loss 0--0.5, A* CARE success is 0.7600, 0.7563, 0.7525, 0.7362,
0.7063 and 0.6863. The sensor extension exposes a necessary context boundary:
CARE--One-shot is 0.0000, $+0.0650$, and $+0.0975$ at
3$\times$3, 5$\times$5, and 7$\times$7. All methods remain at 0.5 under
3$\times$3 because optimistic A* lacks enough locally observed map support;
repair cannot invent that context. Candidate-cap hit rate falls from 0.478 to
0.366 and 0.144 as sensing expands. In the controlled geometry, the peer first
observes the critical obstacle at step zero and commitment occurs at step two
for all footprints, isolating context from deadline length.
At 7$\times$7, A* CARE reaches 0.9375 versus 0.8712 Scuttlebutt, 0.8562
IBLT and 0.8400 Merkle. Merkle still transmits 23.5 mismatching leaves per
episode, while IBLT decodes 87.5\% of sketches at 5$\times$5; hence their
task gap is not an inactive-implementation artifact.

Delay provides a second causal boundary. A* CARE success at one-way delays
0/1/2/4 is 0.7362/0.6913/0.6475/0.6525, while the signed repair window is
2/0/$-2$/$-6$ steps. CARE no longer beats One-shot once the window is
non-positive. Across obstacle densities 0.10/0.20/0.30/0.40, its paired A*
gain is $+0.0950/+0.0650/+0.0537/+0.0175$. It varies substantially by
decision topology ($+0.1537$ on T-junctions, $+0.0112$ on asymmetric forks,
$+0.0213$ on narrow bypasses) and remains positive from 4 to 32 robots. On
generic random maps, three of four density CIs include zero, supporting the
narrower decision-critical claim rather than generic anti-entropy benefit.

Every ablation is paired directly with full CARE. Reducing cadence to $K=4$
loses 0.0362 A* success; lowering the candidate cap from 8 to 4 loses 0.0213.
Using $q=1$ is statistically close but declares a smaller uncertainty family.
Raising the cap to 12 changes success insignificantly and adds 0.93~KB and
about 1.48~s. The frozen $q=2$, cap-8, $K=1$ setting is the selected bounded
traffic--reliability--compute point.

Exact certification is not free. At the primary point CARE takes about
1.04~s per A* episode and 1.14~s per D* Lite episode, versus 0.26/0.37~s for
CARE-Lite. Ratios of 3.99$\times$/3.09$\times$ fail our preregistered 3$\times$
overhead gate. We therefore reject any negligible-overhead claim; bounded
exactness is a measured cost and CARE-Lite remains a practical approximation.

\section{Conclusion}
CARE turns decision-critical replica repair into a codec-priced constrained
communication algorithm. Exact scenario separation produces a minimum action
certificate and route commitment supplies its deadline. Matched A* and D* Lite
results, including three published reconciliation algorithms and two closest
task-aware heuristics, show that path awareness explains most empirical gain.
CARE's narrower contribution is exact bounded joint-scenario separation and a
selective operating point between zero communication and bandwidth-saturating
full synchronization, while sensing,
delay, topology, random-map and compute controls identify where the mechanism
does and does not help. Reliable ARQ remains the higher-traffic reliability
endpoint rather than a target CARE claims to dominate.
