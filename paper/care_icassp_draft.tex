\title{CARE: Repair Before You Decide in Lossy Multi-Robot Map Sharing}
\author{Anonymous Authors}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Sparse remote map updates can be lost precisely when a robot must commit to an
uncertain route. We formulate replica repair as encoded-byte minimization under
a planning deadline and introduce CARE, a versioned anti-entropy protocol that
computes a minimum decision certificate. CARE enumerates a bounded sparse
occupancy-scenario family and solves an exact hitting-set problem: queried cells
must separate every scenario pair inducing different repairable actions. A
second route-commitment certificate accounts for query--patch latency. Across
9,600 complete episodes (100 matched independent maps, six loss rates, eight
policies, A* and D* Lite), CARE forms a reliability--traffic Pareto point. At
30\% loss it improves A* completion success from 0.8400 to 0.9375 for only
2.44~KB additional attempted traffic over one-shot sharing. It matches the
rule-based CARE-Lite ablation while saving 0.83~KB and uses 196~KB less than
reliable ARQ. D* Lite gives 0.9413 success at the same traffic point.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
multi-robot systems, map sharing, lossy communication, replicated state,

\section{Introduction}
Robots with local sensing often exchange map state. A dropped cell update can
leave one receiver with a stale replica; if the cell lies on an imminent route,
the receiver may commit before local sensing resolves the uncertainty. This is
not a general claim that every replica mismatch matters. It is a specific
failure mode of one-shot explicit map sharing under a decision deadline.

We study an explicit-state communication problem rather than replace the
planner or learn a latent communication policy. Learned communication systems
optimize different representations and coordination objectives
\cite{ma2021dcc,wang2023scrimp}. Here all methods share the cell representation,
binary codec, instance, budgets and lossy trace; only missing-state recovery
changes. A* and D* Lite are evaluated independently to separate the protocol
claim from one planner's cache or tie breaking.

Our contributions are: (i) an encoded-byte optimization with an exact
scenario-separation certificate and a necessary-and-sufficient hitting-set
condition; (ii) a dual certificate combining action identifiability with a
deadline derived from route commitment and measured query--patch latency;
(iii) a bounded exact online solver and complexity statement; and (iv) matched
100-map evidence across six loss rates, eight recovery policies and
static/incremental planners, including traffic, reliability, delay and compute.

\section{Decision-Critical Replica Repair}
Each robot maintains a versioned cell map $M_i^t$. A cell stamp orders version,
observation time, sender and state tie break; an incoming cell is merged only
if its stamp dominates the local stamp. Normal observations are directed
one-shot deltas. Data, digest, acknowledgement and repair packets traverse the
same directed lossy channel.

We use an executable fixed-width wire format. A cell delta is 13 bytes, a query
over $n$ stamped cells is $16+6n$ bytes, a patch returning $m$ cells is
$4+13m$ bytes, an ACK is 8 bytes and a replica digest is 16 bytes. Decoders
validate lengths, versions, bounds, reserved bits and checksums. All attempted
encoded bytes, including lost packets, count as traffic.

Let $U_j$ contain at most $n=8$ UNKNOWN vertices on, or one action-graph hop
from, receiver $j$'s next $H=8$ optimistic path vertices. A scenario
$\omega\subseteq U_j$ marks blocked candidates. We enumerate
$\Omega_q=\{\omega:|\omega|\leq q\}$ with $q=2$, hence at most
$S=\sum_{r=0}^q{n\choose r}=37$ scenarios. The all-free scenario uses the
operational path; a canonical shortest-path oracle evaluates blocked
counterfactuals on the planner-shared graph, yielding $\pi_\omega$.
For a pair with different sequences, let $\delta(\omega,\omega')$ be its first
action divergence and $D_{\omega\omega'}=\omega\triangle\omega'$. The
query--patch round trip is $L_{\rm rtt}=2d$. Retaining pairs with
$\delta(\omega,\omega')\geq L_{\rm rtt}$ gives feasible conflict set
$\mathcal E_j$. With query cost $B(Q)=16+6|Q|$, CARE solves
\begin{equation}
\min_{Q\subseteq U_j} B(Q)\quad\mathrm{s.t.}\quad
Q\cap D_e\neq\varnothing,\quad \forall e\in\mathcal E_j .
\label{eq:hitting}
\end{equation}

\textit{Proposition.} $Q$ identifies every feasible action class in
$\Omega_q$ iff it satisfies (\ref{eq:hitting}). Query observations under two
scenarios are identical iff $Q\cap(\omega\triangle\omega')=\varnothing$;
therefore hitting every action-conflicting pair is respectively necessary and
sufficient. Since each entry costs six bytes, minimum cardinality is minimum
encoded size. Cardinality-ordered enumeration is exact with bounded cost
$O(SP+S^2H+2^n|\mathcal E_j|n)$, where $P$ is one bounded path search.

Latency adds a route-entry deadline. Let $\pi_j^+$ traverse UNKNOWN and
$\pi_j^-$ block it, and let $k_j$ be the first UNKNOWN index on $\pi_j^+$.
The safe slack is $\tau_j=\max(0,k_j-1)$. When
$L_{\rm rtt}\leq\tau_j$, a one-hop influence certificate is formed between
the paths' divergence and reconvergence; otherwise it is suppressed. At zero
delay, CARE uses the exact scenario certificate. With positive delay it sends
the duplicate-free union of scenario and route-commitment certificates. A peer
returns only cells with dominating stamps. The guarantee is exact only within
the declared bounded $q$-sparse family; it neither accesses map truth nor
assumes a peer knows each queried cell.

\section{Experimental Protocol}
The task is an eight-robot shared-map decision geometry: remote observers can
see a blocked branch before paired seekers reach a fork, while seekers cannot
locally observe it before committing. Every condition uses 100 independently
generated maps, paired across policies with the same independent loss trace and
per-step budgets. The complete loss/baseline matrix contains 9,600 episodes:
six i.i.d. packet-loss probabilities from 0 to 0.5, eight policies and two
planners. We report means, sample standard deviations and deterministic
20,000-draw map-cluster bootstrap 95\% intervals. Paired comparisons subtract
outcomes within the same map and trace before resampling.

Controls are One-shot Unique Delta, Retry-All ARQ, Path-Weighted ARQ, Periodic
Full Anti-Entropy ($K=4$), Mismatch-Triggered Full Repair, fixed-corridor
PSR-UT, rule-based CARE-Lite and final dual-certificate CARE. Existing
single-factor ablations vary trigger, cadence and repair scope. Extensions vary
obstacle density, link delay, agent/map scale and decision topology, with
random maps as negative controls.

\section{Results}
At 30\% loss with A*, CARE improves One-shot completion success by $+0.0975$
(paired 95\% CI $[+0.0788,+0.1163]$) for 2.44~KB additional attempted bytes.
Relative to PSR-UT, success changes by $+0.0050$
($[-0.0125,+0.0225]$) while saving 7.98~KB ($[-8.20,-7.76]$).
Relative to CARE-Lite, success is unchanged ($[-0.0138,+0.0138]$) and CARE
saves 0.83~KB ($[-1.00,-0.66]$). D* Lite independently gives $+0.0950$ over
One-shot and 0.9413 CSR at 67.67~KB. CARE does not claim universal dominance:
Path-Weighted ARQ reaches 0.9775 CSR at 263.9~KB, while Periodic Full reaches
0.9575 at 285.8~KB. CARE is the compact 0.9375/67.8~KB operating point.

CARE's A* CSR across loss 0.1--0.5 is 0.9888, 0.9788, 0.9375,
0.9013 and 0.8263. Its paired gain over One-shot remains positive at every
nonzero loss rate, from $+0.0262$ to $+0.0975$, and the D* Lite trend agrees.
At loss 0.5, CARE trades 0.0200 A* CSR against CARE-Lite for 1.23~KB less
traffic; thus the certificate is a tradeoff, not uniformly superior.

The prior ablations explain the gain. Replacing PSR-UT with action-triggered
repair reduces CSR from 0.9325 to 0.8400; changing repair cadence from every
step to every four steps gives 0.8488; full-replica repair gives 0.8400 and
174.0~KB. The deadline sweep supplies a causal check: with two delivery steps,
about half the repairs remain feasible and CARE-Lite reaches 0.8550 while
saving 11.78~KB versus PSR-UT; at four steps no query can meet the derived
deadline, so repair is suppressed.

In the 32-worker loss matrix, exact certification takes 0.77~s per 25-step A*
episode and 0.87~s with D* Lite, respectively 2.52$\times$ and 2.09$\times$
CARE-Lite episode CPU. Across 4/8/16/32-agent scale controls, PSR-UT's paired
gain over One-shot is positive but small. In three independent fork topologies
it is $+0.1163$ to $+0.1275$, while random maps are a negative control. Replica
repair matters when a missing update changes an imminent route, not for
arbitrary disagreement.

\section{Conclusion}
CARE turns decision-critical replica repair into a codec-priced constrained
communication algorithm. Exact scenario separation derives a minimum action
certificate, route commitment derives a latency certificate, and their union
protects decisions before execution. Matched A* and D* Lite results across six
loss rates support a planner-independent reliability--traffic operating point.
Reliable ARQ remains the higher-traffic reliability endpoint rather than a
target CARE claims to dominate.
